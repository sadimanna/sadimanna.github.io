Who I Am

I am an ANRF Postdoctoral Fellow at the Indian Institute of Science (IISc), Bangalore, working in the Department of Computational and Data Sciences with Prof. Anirban Chakraborty. Prior to IISc, I was a Senior Research Assistant at Hong Kong Baptist University with Prof. Yiu-Ming Cheung, and completed my Ph.D. in Computer Science at the Indian Statistical Institute (ISI), Kolkata, under the supervision of Prof. Umapada Pal.

Research Vision

My overarching goal is to build privacy-preserving, adversarial-resilient distributed AI systems. When healthcare providers or autonomous agents collaborate across institutions, sharing parameters must not compromise proprietary data. I investigate how parameter-efficient adapters (PEFT) and foundation models can be secured against inversion attacks, poisoning, and information leakage without sacrificing convergence speed.

Research Trajectory (How the pieces connect)

  1. Foundation (Ph.D. at ISI Kolkata)Self-supervised representation learning and label-efficient medical image analysis.

  3. Current Frontier (Postdoc at IISc)AI security, gradient inversion attacks, and privacy vulnerabilities in parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) adapters.

Research Areas

Integrated research domains bridging distributed optimization, threat analysis, representation learning, and medical AI.

  • Federated Learning

    Developing resilient distributed learning algorithms that train collaboratively across decentralized, non-IID client partitions with parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) and adaptive aggregation.

    • Distributed Optimization
    • PEFT Adapters
    • Personalization
    • Non-IID Heterogeneity

  • AI Security & Privacy

    Auditing and fortifying learning systems against adversarial threats, including gradient inversion attacks, prompt poisoning in foundation models, and verifiable machine unlearning.

    • Gradient Inversion Attacks
    • Prompt Poisoning
    • Machine Unlearning
    • Privacy Guarantees

  • Self-Supervised Representation Learning

    Advancing contrastive and generative pretext objectives that acquire transferable, sample-efficient visual representations from unlabeled and multimodal data.

    • Contrastive Learning
    • Mutual Information
    • Vision Transformers
    • Foundation Models

  • Medical AI & Clinical Diagnostics

    Bridging machine learning theory with clinical healthcare, focusing on label-efficient organ segmentation, rare foreground imbalance, and safe diagnostic assistance.

    • Medical Image Analysis
    • Few-Shot Segmentation
    • Brachytherapy Tracking
    • Clinical AI

Current Research Questions

  1. How do parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) adapters interact with gradient inversion vulnerabilities during federated model synchronization?
  2. Can decentralized vision-language models maintain robustness against prompt poisoning and adversarial manipulation across heterogeneous clients?
  3. How can self-supervised representation learning guarantee label-efficiency in clinical imaging without succumbing to severe domain shift?
  4. How can we reconcile client personalization with formal differential privacy and Byzantine fault tolerance in distributed optimization?

Long-Term Research Goals

Trustworthy Distributed AI
Establish distributed learning frameworks whose empirical performance matches centralized baselines while offering verifiable security against gradient leakage.
Sample-Efficient Representation Learning
Devise self-supervised pretext tasks that transfer seamlessly across multimodal domains with minimal manual curation.
Secure Cross-Institutional Collaboration
Enable hospitals and research institutions to train state-of-the-art shared models without transmitting raw patient data.
High-Stakes Clinical Translation
Deploy robust, interpretable computer vision algorithms for real-world clinical interventions in oncology and reproductive health.
