<p>In this article, we will discuss the major contributions of the paper “Big Self-Supervised Models are Strong Semi-Supervised Learners” which is simply termed as SimCLRv2, an improved version of SimCLR.</p>
<h2 id="major-changes-made-to-simclr"><a class="heading-anchor" aria-hidden tabindex="-1" href="#major-changes-made-to-simclr"><span class="anchor-icon">#</span></a>Major Changes Made to SimCLR</h2>
<ol>
<li><strong>Larger ResNet models</strong>: The largest model used in SimCLR was ResNet50(x4). In SimCLRv2, the authors pre-trained ResNet152(x3) with and without selective kernel (+SK) convolutional layers.</li>
<li><strong>Increase capacity of Projection layer</strong>: The capacity of the non-linear network <em><strong>g</strong></em>(·) (a.k.a. projection head) is also increased, by making it deeper. Furthermore, instead of throwing away g(·) entirely after pretraining as in SimCLR, the authors fine-tune from a middle layer. This small change yields a significant improvement for both linear evaluation and fine-tuning with only a few labelled examples.</li>
<li><strong>Memory Bank</strong>: The authors also incorporate the memory mechanism from MoCo, which designates a memory network (with a moving average of weights for stabilization) whose output will be buffered as negative examples. However, this addition does not improve the performance much (~1%) in linear evaluation.</li>
</ol>
<h2 id="key-findings"><a class="heading-anchor" aria-hidden tabindex="-1" href="#key-findings"><span class="anchor-icon">#</span></a>Key Findings</h2>
<ol>
<li><strong>Bigger self-supervised models are more label efficient</strong>: Empirical results suggest that for semi-supervised learning (via the task-agnostic use of unlabeled data), the fewer the labels, the more it is possible to benefit from a bigger model. Bigger self-supervised models are more label efficient, performing significantly better when fine-tuned on only a few labelled examples, even though they have more capacity to potentially overfit.</li>
<li><strong>Extra capacity not always necessary</strong>: Although big models are important for learning general (visual) representations, the extra capacity may not be necessary when a specific target task is concerned. Therefore, with the task-specific use of unlabeled data, the predictive performance of the model can be further improved and transferred into a smaller network.</li>
<li><strong>Bigger/Deeper Projection Heads Improve Representation Learning</strong>: A deeper projection head not only improves the representation quality measured by linear evaluation but also improves semi-supervised performance when fine-tuning from a middle layer of the projection head.</li>
<li><strong>Distillation Using Unlabeled Data Improves Semi-Supervised Learning</strong>: Distillation with unlabeled examples improves fine-tuned models in two ways: (1) when the student model has a smaller architecture than the teacher model, it improves the model efficiency by transferring task-specific knowledge to a student model, (2) even when the student model has the same architecture as the teacher model (excluding the projection head after ResNet encoder), self-distillation can still meaningfully improve the semi-supervised learning performance. To obtain the best performance for smaller ResNets, the big model is self-distilled before distilling it into smaller models.</li>
</ol>
