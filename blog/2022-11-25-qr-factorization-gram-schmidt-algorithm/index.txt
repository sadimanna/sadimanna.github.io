<p><strong>QR Factorization</strong> is a method to decompose any matrix M with <strong>linearly independent columns</strong>, as the <strong>product of two matrices Q and R</strong>, where Q is a matrix with <strong>orthonormal columns</strong> and R is an <strong>upper triangular matrix with non-zero diagonal elements</strong>.</p>
<p><img src="/images/blog/qr-factorization-gram-schmidt-algorithm/img2.png" alt=""><img src="/images/blog/qr-factorization-gram-schmidt-algorithm/img3.png" alt=""></p>
<p>such that</p>
<p><img src="/images/blog/qr-factorization-gram-schmidt-algorithm/img4.png" alt=""><img src="/images/blog/qr-factorization-gram-schmidt-algorithm/img5.png" alt=""></p>
<p>M has dimensions (m, n). Hence, Q has dimensions (m, n) and R has dimensions (n, n). If <strong>m = n</strong>, then Q is orthogonal.</p>
<h3 id="gram-schmidt-algorithm"><a class="heading-anchor" aria-hidden tabindex="-1" href="#gram-schmidt-algorithm"><span class="anchor-icon">#</span></a>Gram-Schmidt Algorithm</h3>
<p>This algorithm is used to determine if a list of <strong>m</strong>-vectors a1,<br>
 . . . , ak is linearly independent. The length of vector <strong>ak</strong> is <strong>m</strong>.</p>
<p><img src="/images/blog/qr-factorization-gram-schmidt-algorithm/img6.png" alt=""></p>
<p>Source: “Introduction to Applied Linear Algebra Vectors, Matrices, and Least Squares”, Boyd, Vandenberghe</p>
<p>Now, this algorithm is used to calculate the Q and R matrices from M column by column under the assumption that the columns of M are linearly independent.</p>
<h3 id="code"><a class="heading-anchor" aria-hidden tabindex="-1" href="#code"><span class="anchor-icon">#</span></a>Code</h3>
<p>To compute <strong>Q</strong> and <strong>R</strong> from <strong>M</strong> using Python, we can follow the following steps.</p>

  <span class="hljs-keyword">for</span> k <span class="hljs-keyword">in</span> <span class="hljs-built_in">range</span>(<span class="hljs-number">1</span>,n+<span class="hljs-number">1</span>):
    c = k-<span class="hljs-number">1</span>
    R[:k,[c]] = Q[:,:k].T @ M[:,[c]] 
    v = M[:,[c]] — Q[:,:k] @ R[:k,[c]] 
    R[c,c] = np.linalg.norm(v)
    Q[:,[c]] = v / R[c,c] 
    print_TableForm(Q, ‘Q’, k)
    print_TableForm(R, ‘R’, k)
