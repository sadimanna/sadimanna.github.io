<p><em>Both the Inception and Residual networks are SOTA architectures, which have shown very good performance with relatively low computational cost. Inception-ResNet combines the two architectures to further boost the performance.</em></p>
<h3 id="residual-inception-blocks"><a class="heading-anchor" aria-hidden tabindex="-1" href="#residual-inception-blocks"><span class="anchor-icon">#</span></a><strong>Residual Inception blocks</strong></h3>
<p><img src="/images/blog/building-inception-resnet-v2-in-keras-from-scratch/img2.png" alt=""></p>
<p>Residual Inception Block(Inception-ResNet-A)</p>
<ol>
<li>Each Inception block is followed by a filter expansion layer <br>
(1 × 1 convolution without activation) which is used for scaling up the dimensionality of the filter bank before the addition to match the depth of the input.</li>
<li>In the case of Inception-ResNet, batch-normalization is used only on top of the traditional layers, but not on top of the summations.</li>
</ol>
<h4 id="scaling-of-residuals"><a class="heading-anchor" aria-hidden tabindex="-1" href="#scaling-of-residuals"><span class="anchor-icon">#</span></a>Scaling of Residuals</h4>
<p>According to the authors, if the number of filters exceeded 1000, the residual variants started to exhibit instabilities and the network has just “died” early in the training, meaning that the last layer before the average pooling started to produce only zeros after a few tens of thousands of iterations. This could not be prevented, neither by lowering the learning rate, nor by adding an extra batchnormalization to this layer.</p>
<p><img src="/images/blog/building-inception-resnet-v2-in-keras-from-scratch/img3.png" alt=""></p>
<p>Scaling of Residuals</p>
<p>According to them, scaling down the residuals before adding them to the previous layer activation seemed to stabilize the training. To scale the residuals, scaling factors between <strong>0.1</strong> and <strong>0.3</strong> were picked.</p>
<p><img src="/images/blog/building-inception-resnet-v2-in-keras-from-scratch/img4.png" alt=""></p>
<p><em>The above image has been taken from</em> <a href="https://ai.googleblog.com/2016/08/improving-inception-and-image.html"><em>here</em></a><em>.</em></p>

<h4 id="image-pre-processing"><a class="heading-anchor" aria-hidden tabindex="-1" href="#image-pre-processing"><span class="anchor-icon">#</span></a>Image Pre-processing</h4>
<pre><code class="hljs language-python"><span class="hljs-keyword">def</span> <span class="hljs-title function_">random_crop</span>(<span class="hljs-params">img, random_crop_size</span>): 
    <span class="hljs-comment"># Note: image_data_format is ‘channel_last’ </span>
    h, w = img.shape[<span class="hljs-number">0</span>], img.shape[<span class="hljs-number">1</span>] 
    dy, dx = random_crop_size 
    x = np.random.randint(<span class="hljs-number">0</span>, w — dx + <span class="hljs-number">1</span>) 
    y = np.random.randint(<span class="hljs-number">0</span>, h — dy + <span class="hljs-number">1</span>) 
    <span class="hljs-keyword">return</span> img[y:(y+dy), x:(x+dx), :]
</code></pre>
<pre><code class="hljs language-python"><span class="hljs-keyword">def</span> <span class="hljs-title function_">crop_generator</span>(<span class="hljs-params">batches, crop_length</span>): 
    “””Take <span class="hljs-keyword">as</span> <span class="hljs-built_in">input</span> a Keras ImageGen (Iterator) 
    <span class="hljs-keyword">and</span> generate random crops <span class="hljs-keyword">from</span> the image batches 
    generated by the original iterator. “”” 
    sz = crop_length 
    <span class="hljs-keyword">while</span> <span class="hljs-literal">True</span>: 
        batch_x, batch_y = <span class="hljs-built_in">next</span>(batches) 
        batch_crops = np.zeros((batch_x.shape[<span class="hljs-number">0</span>], sz, sz, <span class="hljs-number">3</span>)) 
        <span class="hljs-keyword">for</span> i <span class="hljs-keyword">in</span> <span class="hljs-built_in">range</span>(batch_x.shape[<span class="hljs-number">0</span>]): 
            batch_crops[i] = random_crop(batch_x[i], (sz,sz))
        <span class="hljs-keyword">yield</span> (batch_crops, batch_y)
</code></pre>
